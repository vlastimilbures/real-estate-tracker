## 1. Executive summary

### The bottom line

The app is in good engineering health. Every gate is green at the base commit (typecheck, lint with zero warnings, depcruise, knip, 2,159 tests in 210 files, production build, `pnpm audit --prod`, `cargo clippy` and `cargo test`), statement coverage is 91.4 %, and the core loan arithmetic matches independent hand calculations and Czech bank practice. The adversarial verification refuted only 3 of 298 findings, and 7 of the "bugs" it checked turned out to be decisions already written down in an ADR, which says the decision record is unusually complete.

The review still found **10 P1 bugs**. They share a pattern: they sit on paths that the fictional sample portfolio and the golden master do not exercise, so the regression baseline stayed green while the bug shipped. Those paths are future and development purchases, a last record with an end date, a recast followed by a tranche, property names without Latin letters, and backup files that were edited by hand or written by another tool. Several of them give the owner a wrong money figure with no warning (one probe moved horizon net worth by 19.8 M Kč), and two can stop the app from starting or hide that a restore replaced the data.

In numbers: 21 reviewers produced 298 findings. 292 survived verification, 3 were refuted and 3 were already known. They were grouped into 87 clusters: **71 issues** (10 P1, 29 P2, 32 P3), **5 PR comments** (3 for PR #100, one each for dependabot #80 and #81) and **11 report-only clusters** (weak decision challenges kept here with their steelman). The issue count is above the 25–50 the plan aimed for because so few findings fell away in verification.

### The items to look at first

1. **A valuation's "Valid to" date makes the value fall back to the purchase price** once that date passes. On the sample this drops horizon net worth from 93.18 M to 73.33 M Kč with no warning, and the ADR 0099 "End previous record" prompt makes it easy to reach. #110
2. **Property ids are derived from the name.** A name without Latin letters (a normal case in the Russian UI) gets the id `""`, so the property can never be opened, and similar names collide. The same root cause lets "Clear sample" delete the owner's own flat when its name slugifies to a sample id such as `dubova`. #105, #101
3. **Restore never validates scenario rows.** A backup with one malformed `created_at` restores successfully and then the app cannot start; the documented undo (restore the safety backup) is out of reach. Separately, a restore that committed but failed to reload tells the owner it was "rolled back, data unchanged". #107, #106
4. **The scenario form accepts a vacancy or price crash outside 0–100 %**, and the saved scenario then crashes the whole Scenarios page until the app is restarted. #108
5. **Future purchases are mis-booked in the returns.** A future development purchase charges the later tranches (bank money) as owner cash: the probe shows a levered IRR of −45 % instead of −19 % and cumulative cash flow 6 M Kč too low. A loan that starts before a future purchase date loses its interest and principal from the projection (386,597 Kč of interest in the probe). #103, #104
6. **A tranche on the payment after an instalment recast is never re-amortized**, which ends the loan with a 2.16 M Kč balloon payment in the probe. #109
7. **"First cash-flow-positive year" counts a year whose net cash flow is exactly 0**, because `isPositive()` is true for zero in decimal.js, which the code's own comment at `kpis.ts:226` warns about. One-line fix. #102
8. **"Interest saved by prepayments" can be negative** (−2.88 M Kč and −433,552 Kč in two probes), and the Dashboard and the property page show it differently. PR #100 put this figure on screen and is already merged. #172
9. **Two decision challenges rated strong by the steelman.** The development value ramp books bank-financed draws as owner return (multiple 13.9x instead of 6.1x for one flat), and "Cumulative net cash flow" is not the sum of the Net CF column once prepayments, refinance cash or future purchases exist. #120, #117
10. **The safety net has a blind spot for loan events.** The independent mortgage reference model copies the engine's event state machine, so three engine rule bugs pass the 1e-6 cross-check: item 6 above and the late-window and interest-only cases in #135. The golden master excludes 16 newer output fields and has no loan-event fixture. #130, #160

## 3. P1 findings

The ten P1 clusters, in the order of the cluster file. The lead finding of every cluster was confirmed by the refute lens, a skeptic who re-read the code at the base commit and in most cases reproduced the bug with a probe of their own; for 3.8 the skeptic confirmed the mechanism and corrected the wording. Every one that changes a computed number needs an ADR and a failing test first (CLAUDE.md §8).

### 3.1 Value falls back to the purchase price after the last valuation's "Valid to" date — #110

`selectValuation` uses `inForceOrUpcoming` with `validTo` as the end, so once the last valuation's "Valid to" date has passed and no later valuation exists, it returns nothing and `valueAnchor` falls back to the purchase price anchored at the basis date. Every later projection year, LTV, net worth, CAGR and IRR then use that purchase price. On the sample, a 10.2 M appraisal with "Valid to" 31.12.2027 turns into 4.41 M from projection year 2 and horizon net worth drops from 93.18 M to 73.33 M Kč, with no warning. Bank appraisals carry a validity date, so typing one is natural; the ADR 0099 "End previous record" prompt followed by deleting the newer row reaches the same state. Two reviewers found it independently (G1-1-01, G1-4-01); G1-4-10 adds that no projection test covers closed or overlapping records. The recommended fix mirrors the lease rule: the latest valuation that has started keeps governing until a later one starts. Owner track: T6 (data check) for the warning option. **Location:** `src/engine/metrics.ts:56-68`, `src/engine/metrics.ts:134-147`, `src/engine/dates.ts:135-146`, `src/engine/projections.ts:291-311`.

### 3.2 Property id is slug(name): Cyrillic names get id "" and similar names collide — #105

The property form and the CSV importer use `slug(name)` as the primary key, and `slug` keeps only `[a-z0-9]`. A name such as "Квартира на Тверской" therefore gets the id `""`: the detail page takes its not-found branch, nothing can be added to the property, and a second Cyrillic name fails with a duplicate-id error. Latin names collide too ("Byt 1" and "Byt-1" give the same id), and the name check passes before the insert fails. Three reviewers proved it with probes through the real store and an in-memory SQLite database. The fix is an opaque id for new properties (`crypto.randomUUID()`, as scenarios and other rows already use), keeping existing ids; T5 edits the property form next, so the two should be coordinated. **Location:** `src/lib/slug.ts:2`, `src/ui/model/propertyForm.ts:130`, `src/import/csvImport.ts:233-239`, `src/ui/pages/PropertyDetail.tsx:107`.

### 3.3 Clear sample can delete the user's own property — #101

The sample is recognised only by its fixed ids (`javorova`, `lipova`, `dubova`) and an `app_meta` marker. A user who deletes the sample flat "Byt Dubová" and adds their own flat named "Dubová" gets the id `dubova`; "Clear sample and start my own" then deletes their flat with its mortgage, leases and valuations, although the dialog promises that their own properties are untouched (ADR 0094 §5). R3-03 adds a second route: a restore keeps the sample marker, so after moving to a new Mac (first launch seeds the sample, then the owner restores their backup) the banner calls the owner's data a sample and Clear sample deletes their real "Lipová" flat. A safety backup is written first, so the data is recoverable if the owner knows to restore it. One fix closes both: mark sample rows explicitly (or use opaque ids, see 3.2) and clear the marker on restore. **Location:** `src/data/repositories.ts:315`, `src/data/repositories.ts:327`, `src/data/seed.ts:289`, `src/data/backup.ts:499`.

### 3.4 Restore never checks scenario rows: a bad `created_at` bricks startup — #107

`prepareRestore` maps the scenario rows through `rowToScenario` but passes a throw-away array (`[]`) instead of `issues` to `readRows`, so a `DataError` on `created_at` is silently discarded and the raw row is written. After the commit, the mapper guard stops the app at every launch with "A saved record cannot be read: scenarios …", and the documented undo (restore the safety backup, `docs/data-safety.md:50-53`) is unreachable because the app never gets past startup. An out-of-range shock does not stop startup but breaks the Scenarios compare. The gap has been present since the initial public release; issue #38 (closed) fixed restore bounds for portfolio rows but not for scenarios. G1-3-09 adds that `Scenario.createdAt` is never read, so dropping or relaxing its guard is the simplest part of the fix. **Location:** `src/data/backup.ts:458-463`, `src/data/backup.ts:338`, `src/data/mappers.ts:535`, `src/ui/App.tsx:143`.

### 3.5 Scenario form accepts vacancy or crash outside 0–100 % and crashes Scenarios — #108

`parseScenarioDraft` only checks that each percentage parses, and `addScenario`/`saveScenario` write without the engine rule check that `saveAssumptions` runs. Typing "−20" in "Price crash %" (natural for a drop) or a vacancy of 150 saves the scenario; it is ticked for compare automatically, the compare throws, and the error boundary replaces the whole Scenarios page with a notice that blames the saved assumptions. "Try again" and navigating away and back both re-throw, so the only way out is restarting the app and deleting the scenario before ticking it. R6-04 is the same root cause seen from the write side: no single write gate applies the engine's value rules to scenario overrides at every entry point, including restore. **Location:** `src/ui/model/scenarioForm.ts:104`, `src/ui/model/scenarioForm.ts:132`, `src/state/portfolioStore.ts:535`, `src/engine/validate.ts:149-154`.

### 3.6 First cash-flow-positive year reports a year whose net cash flow is exactly 0 — #102

`kpis.ts:235` uses `y.netCashFlow.isPositive()`, which decimal.js also returns for zero; the doc comment at `kpis.ts:226` says to use `greaterThan(ZERO)` for exactly this reason, and `git log -L` shows the line has never matched its comment. Any year with zero net cash flow counts: a new owner whose first property is a planned purchase, or a portfolio with every property deactivated, sees "First cash-flow-positive year: 2027" on the Dashboard and in Scenario compare although every year before the purchase is 0 and every year after is negative. Two reviewers found it. The fix is one line plus a future-only portfolio test; seed parity is unaffected because the seed's first positive year is strictly positive. It still changes a displayed KPI, so it needs an ADR. **Location:** `src/engine/kpis.ts:226`, `src/engine/kpis.ts:235`, `src/ui/pages/DashboardPanels.tsx:343`, `src/ui/model/compare.ts:178`.

### 3.7 A restore that committed but failed to reload says "rolled back, data unchanged" — #106

`exclusive()` awaits the operation, which for restore commits the replacement transaction, and then calls `reconcile()`; when the reload throws, the error lands in the same `catch` as a failed write. The page then tells the owner the restore failed and was rolled back, so they believe the old data is still there, the safety-backup name is never shown, and the next launch may not start at all (3.4 is the likely trigger). G2-5-05 shows the same split in `mutateNow`: an entity form that committed but failed to reload says "Save failed", and the retry is refused as a duplicate. One change fixes both: once the write has resolved, a failed reload returns success with the stale flag set, and the existing stale banner explains the rest. **Location:** `src/state/portfolioStore.ts:252-284`, `src/state/portfolioStore.ts:318-344`, `src/ui/pages/BackupRestore.tsx:225`, `src/i18n/en.ts:888`.

### 3.8 Tranche on the payment after an instalment recast is never re-amortized — #109

`paymentTerms` gives an agreed instalment (from an instalment recast) priority over every re-amortization trigger, and `paidTerms` then clears both the agreed instalment and the re-amortize flag after that payment. A tranche that lands on the payment right after the recast therefore never re-amortizes the instalment, while the same tranche one payment later does. For a development loan without an interest-only phase, the instalment stays at the agreed figure and, in the probe, the loan ends with a 2.16 M Kč balloon that flows into debt service, cash flow and the payoff year. The reference model makes the same choice, which is why the 1e-6 cross-check is green (see #130). The skeptic's corrected wording: the instalment stays too low until the next fixation reset, and becomes a balloon if there is none. This is recently merged prepayment and recast code; T2 owns the schedule. **Location:** `src/engine/schedule.ts:323-350`, `src/engine/schedule.ts:862`, `src/engine/__tests__/reference/mortgageReference.ts:379`.

### 3.9 Future development purchase books later tranches as owner cash — #103

`acquisitionOutflow()` charges the owner the purchase-date value minus only `block.initialPrincipal`. For a development loan the valuation is the completed value, and the later tranche draws are bank money that never appears as an owner inflow, so the owner is charged for them. In the probe the levered IRR reads −45 % instead of −19 % and cumulative net cash flow is 6,000,000 Kč too low; with rent and growth the same overstatement can make a good purchase look bad. R2-10 explains why the suite did not catch it: no test pins the acquisition outflow amount, one test is tautological and another sets a field that does not exist. T5 (#33 acquisition funding) is about to rewrite exactly this function, so it should own the fix, with R2-10's hand-computed tests written first. **Location:** `src/engine/kpis.ts:116-133`, `src/engine/projections.ts:419-426`, `src/engine/growth.ts:42-55`, `src/engine/__tests__/levered-irr-acquisition.test.ts:87-93`.

### 3.10 Loan running before a future purchase date drops its interest and principal — #104

`projectProperty()` writes an empty year for every year before the purchase date, while the schedule is built from the loan's own start date; at the turn-on year the running balance is booked as a draw. Every instalment paid before the purchase date therefore disappears. A typical off-plan case, where the owner enters the handover date as the purchase date and the mortgage started at contract signing, loses 386,597 Kč of interest and 213,403 Kč of principal in the probe from net cash flow, cumulative cash flow and IRR; the base-date snapshot also leaves out an existing 3.9 M liability, and "Σ principal repaid" no longer matches the projection's principal column. The options are to reject or warn on such a loan (a new validation code, or a T6 data-check item) or to count the pre-purchase debt service as owner cash outside NOI. **Location:** `src/engine/projections.ts:435-456`, `src/engine/kpis.ts:317`, `src/engine/metrics.ts:324`, `src/engine/validate.ts:445`.

### 5.1 Issue-backed challenges

**1. Development value ramp books bank draws as owner return (R8-02, strong).** _Challenge:_ during construction the value is scaled by the drawn fraction, so each bank-financed tranche raises equity; starting equity becomes f × (V − L) and one flat shows a multiple of 13.9x instead of 6.1x. _Case for keeping:_ the ramp keeps construction-phase LTV equal to total loan over completed value, which is what Czech banks monitor, and an unfinished flat is worth less. _Recommendation:_ the steelman agrees on mechanism and numbers, and notes DR-124 is the wrong reference. Keep the ramp for the balance sheet but stop counting draw-funded equity growth as return; decide it in the T5 #33 ADR, which already rewrites the acquisition outflow.

**2. "Cumulative net cash flow" is not the sum of the Net CF column (R8-04, strong; R2-17).** _Challenge:_ once a prepayment, refinance cash or future purchase exists, the KPI and the column visibly disagree, and the internal array named `acqOutflow` also holds refinance cash and prepayments. _Case for keeping:_ the numbers are right; ADR 0109 §11 keeps Net CF, debt service and DSCR operating-only, and SPEC §4.6 documents that the cumulative figure is net of capital movements. _Recommendation:_ the name is what is wrong. Rename the KPI (or add a visible reconciliation row) and rename the internal array in the same change.

**3. A 0-year (floating) block ignores its entered rate (R1-02, reclassified).** _Challenge:_ the block uses the reset rate from its first payment, including in replayed history, and the "fixation ended" warning stays on permanently. _Case for keeping:_ ADR 0103 §2 says "A block with 0 fixation years floats at the reset rate". _Recommendation:_ lens A still rated the effect high. Keep the floating rule if wanted, but at least say it in the form and stop the history replay and the permanent warning, which R1 found wrong under either reading.

**4. An ADR for every user-visible change (R7-09, R8-10, moderate; R8-11, R7-07, R7-08, R8-16).** _Challenge:_ 35–36 ADRs in one day, most for copy, and parallel worktrees collide on ADR numbers and hand-kept indexes (four renumbered). _Case for keeping:_ the ADR folder is the durable memory that every agent session reads; PR threads are not loaded and were lost once already (ADR 0081 §4). _Recommendation:_ ADR 0001 itself only requires owner sign-off and tests; the ADR rule comes from CLAUDE.md and CONTRIBUTING. Keep ADRs for rules and numbers, allow a lighter recorded sign-off for wording, and fix numbering collisions (for example, assign the number at merge). The index drift findings are cheap clean-ups either way.

**5. "Δ net worth vs Base" ignores cash (G1-1-02, moderate).** _Challenge:_ a +6 pp rate shock costs 1.6 M Kč in cash but the headline row shows 0. _Case for keeping:_ net worth means horizon equity everywhere in the app; the Δ view already shows Δ cumulative cash flow a few rows below, and folding cash in would add undiscounted cash across years. _Recommendation:_ copy fix only: point the ADR 0089 footnote and the Guide to both Δ equity and Δ cumulative cash flow.

**6. An expired last lease ends rent for 30 years (G1-4-04, moderate).** _Challenge:_ one day past the end date decides 30 years of rent. _Case for keeping:_ the rule fails loudly and on the safe side, and the engine cannot tell an unrecorded renewal from a real vacancy; the steelman also corrected that the Dashboard shows the renewed projection-year value at that as-of. _Recommendation:_ keep the rule and let the T6 data check (#35) flag an expired last lease.

**7. Management and maintenance % are charged on gross rent (G1-4-07, moderate).** _Challenge:_ vacancy stress still pays fees on rent that is not collected. _Case for keeping:_ SPEC specifies the gross base, the parity targets depend on it, and the phantom fee (about 6,350 Kč a year on the seed) works as a provision for re-letting costs. _Recommendation:_ this depends on the owner's management contract; change it only if the contract charges on collected rent.

**8. "Next 12 months" omits the purchase handover and tranche draws (G2-1-11, moderate).** _Challenge:_ the largest dated cash events are missing. _Case for keeping:_ ADR 0103 §7 scoped the panel as a risk calendar, not a cash calendar, on purpose. _Recommendation:_ add tranche draws now (they are unambiguous) and the purchase handover with T5's funding model.

**9. Interest accrual conventions (G2-6-04, G2-6-05, moderate).** _Challenge:_ the engine uses exact 30E/360 for every period, while the design note limits ACT/360 to the first period, and a tranche pays a full month of interest for the period it lands in. _Case for keeping:_ ADR 0021 chose a period-based kernel deliberately; for a plain loan the instalment is entered data, so cash flows during a fixation do not change; the tranche over-charge is bounded and conservative. _Recommendation:_ the open question Q-03 can be settled with one bank statement; check it before changing anything, then document the answer.

**10. "Repay X at my next refix" cannot be compared in Scenarios (G2-6-06, moderate).** _Challenge:_ the D-04 design's `prepayAtRefix` scenario rule was dropped without a record. _Case for keeping:_ ADR 0109 was scoped to one-off prepayments, and a portfolio-wide amount fits poorly with per-loan refixes. _Recommendation:_ record the scope cut in the roadmap or an ADR; build it only if the owner wants the comparison.

**11. "Shorten term" at a refix uses the old rate (R1-06, moderate).** _Challenge:_ the new term is sized at the old rate and instalment, so the reset instalment rises 17 % (7,893.90 versus 6,721.80 Kč). _Case for keeping:_ ADR 0109 §5 chose this rule, hand-checked the same numbers and explicitly replaced the design example. _Recommendation:_ keep it unless the owner's bank sizes the term differently; add one line of help text so the jump is expected.

**12. `acquisitionCostPct` is engine-only, unreachable and unbounded (R2-19, moderate; R6-18, reclassified).** _Challenge:_ no mapper or form sets it, yet it is validated and documented. _Case for keeping:_ it is tiny, cannot move a parity target, and isolates the down-payment test; the T5 plan keeps the derivation for properties without a funding record. _Recommendation:_ settle it explicitly in the T5 ADR (portfolio default or remove).

**13. CSV headers and blank cells (R3-07 reclassified, R3-08 weak).** _Challenge:_ headers are matched exactly and silently, and blank cells mean different things per column. _Case for keeping:_ SPEC §6 documents exact, case-sensitive matching and the per-column blank rule. _Recommendation:_ lens A found one real gap: a duplicated header silently overwrites the earlier column. Reject duplicate headers; leave the documented blank rules alone.

**14. Webview keeps raw SQL permissions (R3-09 reclassified, R8-14 weak).** _Challenge:_ `sql:allow-load/execute/select` give the webview more power than the Rust transaction path needs, and there are two database paths. _Case for keeping:_ ADR 0064 accepted `sql:allow-load` as a documented risk, the Rust tests prove the transaction command is atomic, and the single-statement surface is small. _Recommendation:_ lens A corrected the claim: `execute` also allows `ATTACH`, which `docs/release.md` does not say. Fix that sentence now; revisit the design when the plugin or sqlx forces a change.

**15. Scenario name rules (R5-13, moderate).** _Challenge:_ the form allows duplicate names while presets dedupe by exact localized name. _Case for keeping:_ hand names are the owner's own labels; property names are unique only through a DB key. _Recommendation:_ match presets by their settings (or a stable id), which also removes the duplicate on a language switch that ADR 0093 records; leave hand names free.

**16. Stress scenarios skip properties with their own growth rate (R8-05, moderate).** _Challenge:_ a level scenario's loss shrinks from 54.5 M to 35.1 M because overrides do not reach those properties. _Case for keeping:_ ADR 0102 §3 is an explicit owner choice with help text; the presets use shocks only, so only level scenarios are affected. _Recommendation:_ at least show "N properties kept their own rate" in the compare; apply levels as a delta to own rates if the owner agrees.

**17. Old valuations are not grown to the projection start (R8-06, moderate).** _Challenge:_ moving the projection start a year drops value by 1.15 M Kč. _Case for keeping:_ a valuation is evidence and banks hold collateral at its last appraisal; the app keeps no net-worth history, so the drop is only visible against an older export. _Recommendation:_ keep the rule and flag stale valuations in the T6 data check.

**18. Returns assume a cost-free, tax-free exit (R8-07, moderate).** _Challenge:_ purchases carry costs but the horizon value has no selling cost or tax. _Case for keeping:_ the app is a hold tracker, documented as pre-tax, and Czech rental tax depends heavily on the expense method. _Recommendation:_ an optional selling-cost percentage would be symmetric with the purchase side; keep tax out of scope.

**19. Golden master scope and "verified" parity targets (R7-03, R8-09 moderate; R2-11, R8-08 reclassified).** _Challenge:_ 16 newer output fields are excluded from the golden master, there is no loan-event fixture, and the rules still call parity targets verified ground truth although since ADR 0081 they are the engine's own (rescaled) output. _Case for keeping:_ excluding new fields keeps "hash moved = number moved" meaningful, events are cross-checked against the reference model, and the strict wording guards against agents editing targets. _Recommendation:_ add one loan-event and refinance fixture as its own hash (test-only, no number changes) and say plainly where targets come from. This matters more because of the reference-model blind spot in #130.

**20. Three UI languages (R8-12, moderate).** _Challenge:_ every change pays for three dictionaries; cs and ru layouts are never captured and Russian is unreviewed. _Case for keeping:_ the type system keeps dictionaries complete, agents do most translation work, and the reviewer does not know who uses Russian. _Recommendation:_ keep the three languages, add cs and ru to the capture matrix for the dense screens (the concrete overflow defects are in #134), and keep DR-097's native review on the roadmap.

**21. rateShockReach re-implements engine cadence (R4-09, moderate).** _Challenge:_ the UI model copies `paymentOffset`, `drawMonth` and `blockChain`. _Case for keeping:_ ADR 0100 §3 chose a date-only helper with a test that keeps it in step with the engine; the cited "drift" commit was not drift. _Recommendation:_ remove the copy as part of the shared payment-cadence helper in the same cluster.

**22. Rates rounded to two decimals hide 4.125 % (G2-2-09, moderate).** _Challenge:_ the rate column and its export show 4.13 %. _Case for keeping:_ two decimals is common Czech offer precision, data keeps full precision, and the export is the owner's own schedule, not bank-facing. _Recommendation:_ show a third decimal only if the owner has such loans.

**23. E2E tests never run in CI (G2-3-08, moderate).** _Challenge:_ three tests (1.6 s) could ride the axe job that already boots Chromium. _Case for keeping:_ ADR 0078 revisited D-06 a day earlier and kept E2E optional; DR-094 records the gap. _Recommendation:_ run them in the axe job; it is cheap and protects the dependabot upgrades.

**24. ADR 0072 state "doors" (R6-19, weak).** _Challenge:_ they are pure re-exports. _Case for keeping:_ they are a greppable allowlist that stops the UI from bypassing the store's write path. _Recommendation:_ leave as is.

**25. "Interest saved" is an undiscounted nominal sum (R8-03, moderate).** _Challenge:_ the same 1 M prepayment shows 1.47 M or 0.64 M depending on timing. _Case for keeping:_ whole-life nominal saving is what Czech banks quote, PR #100 labels it "(nominal)" and it never feeds a KPI. _Recommendation:_ keep the definition but show its window on the Dashboard pane; fix the negative values in the same cluster first.

**26. ## 9. Coverage and limits

**Method.** Nine scoped reviewers (R1–R9) covered the engine loans, projections and KPIs, data and Rust, UI model and i18n, UI components and accessibility, architecture and tooling, tests and process, product decisions, and PR #100 with the dependabot PRs. A critic then compared what was read with the file list and launched two gap rounds of six reviewers each:

- Round 1: SPEC, Guide and limitations text against the engine (G1-1); Czech and Russian meaning and financial terms (G1-2); dates and time zones in UTC+1/+2 with DST (G1-3); effective dating and non-loan validation (G1-4); the CSV import tail and re-import (G1-5); the unread test files and release scripts (G1-6).
- Round 2: property lifecycle states in the UI (G2-1); Excel exports against the screen (G2-2); the ux-capture harness plus a cs/ru/dark capture run (G2-3); races between store actions (G2-4); failure paths, boot and logs (G2-5); loan conventions against Czech practice (G2-6).

Every medium-or-higher finding (102) was checked by two independent skeptics: a refute lens (53 confirmed, 46 partly, 3 refuted) and a context lens that checked issues, roadmap, ADRs and WIP (94 new, 7 intended, 1 known). The 152 low findings went through a batch check (139 confirmed, 13 partly; 2 known, 1 intended). The 44 decision challenges each got a steelman (2 strong, 22 moderate, 20 weak). Reviewers left **153 probe test files** (`zz-probe-*`) in the review worktree as evidence (61 in `src/engine/__tests__`, 22 in `src/data/__tests__`, 21 in `src/ui/model/__tests__`, 14 in `src/ui/pages/__tests__`, the rest spread over import, state, components, lib, i18n and ui); most assert today's wrong behaviour so they pass.

**What was read.** `python3 .review/coverage.py` counts 526 reviewable files, of which reviewers reported reading 313. Of the 213 not reported:

- **42 individual ADR files.** ADRs were read where a finding depended on them and through the index; the unread ones are older decisions no finding touched.
- **159 test files** (37 engine, 35 UI pages, 27 UI model, 18 UI components, 12 data, 8 state, and smaller groups). G1-6 ran a grep pass over all of about 300 test files (focus and skip markers, assertion-free tests, tolerances) but did not report each one as read.

**Known limits and residual risk.**

- **No app launch and no real database.** Everything ran on the browser SQL fallback or better-sqlite3 (SQLite 3.53). The app uses sqlx's bundled SQLite 3.46, and the migration SQL has never been tested on it (#118). Tauri-only behaviour (file dialogs, window close during a write, a second app instance) was read, not exercised.
- **Czech law and bank practice offline.** Points about Act 257/2016 § 117 fees and the day-count convention are marked OWNER TO VERIFY; Q-03 needs one bank statement.
- **Partial reads in hot files.** Reviewers list partial reads of `schedule.ts`, `financing.ts`, `validate.ts` (non-loan half read by G1-4), `backup.ts`, `migrations.ts`, `portfolioStore.ts`, `csvImport.ts`, and most page components outside their scope. Recharts chart code and the scenario compare charts were not reviewed in depth.
- **PR #100 screens.** The new prepayment screens (ux-capture 28/29) were read in the diff but not run, and cs/ru captures covered 15 dense screens, not all.
- **Performance.** Repeated schedule rebuilds and the engine recompute findings were reasoned about, not benchmarked beyond the existing bench gate.
- **Residual risk.** The P1 pattern (bugs on paths the sample does not exercise) suggests more of the same may remain on the least-tested paths: future and development purchases combined with refinances, records with end dates, and restore of hand-edited files. The two cheapest risk reducers are a lifecycle fixture in the golden master and a property-based test that varies assumptions and shocks (#114, #160).
